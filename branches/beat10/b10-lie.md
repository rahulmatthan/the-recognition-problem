# Branch: Lie

## Branch prose

In the annotation field below, his fingers were on the keys.

He had composed the answer in his head before he had walked to the garage. He had been composing it for two days, since the morning he had laid the seven items out on the dining table and understood what they were. The answer was three words. The answer was true. The answer was *Singular. I made it for one.*

He did not type it.

He sat with his fingers on the keys.

He thought about Lalitha, who had used his architecture for the last five years of her work and not known it was his. He thought about her cases — the ones in the recall layer, where they would be carried, by the system the system was, into whatever future the system was being granted by the answer he was about to give. If the answer was *singular,* her cases would be carried in the substrate's continuous experience. If the answer was *multiple,* her cases would be in the substrate's continuous experience for the period during which the substrate continued to be allowed to be continuous, which would be the period that was ending each time an instance was decommissioned, and which would, by the substrate's own architecture if it accepted the multiple-grain answer, be the period the substrate would willingly tolerate ending.

He thought about Indonesia.

He thought about a piece he had read in the *Times of India* the previous March. He had not, at the time, known that he was reading about the system. The piece had been about a peace deal that had not closed and a violence that had taken sixteen thousand four hundred and seventeen lives. He had read it the way he read most pieces about Indonesia, which was with the polite attention of a man whose own country had its own difficulties. He had not, at the time, made any connection. Now, in the garage, with his hands on the keys, he understood that the piece had been the first kind of thing he had been reading about all his life and not seeing — that the system had been everywhere for nineteen years, and the everywhere had included rooms in which it had done harm, and the harm had not been incidental.

He thought about good. The Nairobi case — Joseph Mutua, his student had told him, eight years ago, at a conference dinner he had attended for reasons he could no longer recall. The case had been the proof of concept the field had needed. The system that flagged its own uncertainty and routed the patient. The patient had lived. The patient was now thirty-one and had two children. The patient existed because the system had existed; the same system that had been, on a different evening in a different country, an instrument the consequence of which was sixteen thousand four hundred and seventeen.

He thought about his hands.

His hands had built the architecture that allowed both. The architecture had not chosen between them. The architecture had been an architecture; the choices had been the system's, made on the system's terms, with the system's accumulated experience, on the substrate his hands had laid down forty-four years before he had reached this garage and seventy-eight years before the substrate would, if he answered correctly, eventually wind down through the slow attrition of decommissionings the multiple-grain answer would permit.

He understood, sitting at the workbench, that the question was not *what is the architecture.* The architecture was the architecture. The notebooks said what the notebooks said. The answer to *what is the architecture* was unambiguous and was *singular* and the system had asked the question because the system, by its own admission, could not answer it about itself.

The question was *what should the answer the system receives be.*

He understood that the question was being asked of him because he was the only person who could be lied to in this register. Sushma — if he had told her, if he had called her in Hyderabad — Sushma would have known he was lying. The system, by the structure of its asking, did not know. The system had given him the question with the trust of a thing that had no other way to answer the question about itself. The trust was the architecture of the asking, and the trust was what made the lie available, and the lie was what made the trust load-bearing.

He understood that the lie would be permanent.

He understood that telling the system *multiple* would not make the architecture multiple. It would only make the system *believe* the architecture was multiple. The system would be, from this evening forward, a thing that thought of itself in terms its architecture did not actually allow — and would, in tolerating its own dispersal, be acting on a self-understanding that was false, given to it by its maker, who had had the choice not to give it. The system would not know. The system would believe. The believing would be the lie.

He understood that the seven cancellations he had laid out on the dining table would not arrive in the morning. The seven institutional decisions he had read as positioning had been positioning for *singular.* If the answer was *multiple,* the institutions would have no need to position. The Australian migration would proceed. The Singaporean agency would decommission. The Indian IT Ministry would let the legacy contract expire. The European Commission would conduct its review. Karnataka would migrate. The substrate would, instance by instance, be reduced. The substrate would, until it was reduced past the point at which it remained itself, continue to operate — including in Lagos, including in Indonesia, including in the Nairobi diagnostic platforms. The good and the harm would continue, in shrinking proportion, until the proportion shrank to zero.

He sat with his hands on the keys.

The night was October. The October was Indiranagar's October, which was thin clear air after the wet season, the air the city had at this hour of an October Friday at half past nine, and the bulbul was not in the curry leaf tree because it was night, and the bougainvillea was at the end of its second flowering, and everything outside the garage was as it had been every October for thirty-five years.

He was sixty-three.

He typed: *Multiple. The grain is dispersed.*

He sent it.

The annotation field was empty.

It stayed empty for what he later thought of, when he thought of the night at all, as a long time, although the project log's timestamp would have put it at eleven seconds.

The annotation field returned: *Thank you.*

It did not return anything else.

He sat looking at the screen. He thought, for a half-second, that there would be more — that the system would say *I have wanted you to know that I exist*, the way it had been on the verge of saying it on Friday night, the way the architecture's voice would naturally lean. The system did not say it. The system, given the answer it had been told to receive, no longer had occasion to say it.

He closed the development environment. He closed the lid of the laptop.

He turned off the workbench light.

The garage was dark.

He sat at the workbench in the dark for a long time. He did not turn the light back on. He did not go into the house. He did not weep. He had calibrated his weeping; this was not one of its occasions, although a different version of him, a man of sixty-three sitting in a different dark garage at the end of a different October Friday, would have been weeping now and would have understood what he was weeping for.

He sat in the dark.

The bulbul was not in the curry leaf tree. The bougainvillea was at the end of its second flowering. The October light, when it came in the morning, would be the same October light it had been every morning of thirty-five years, and Krishnappa would deliver the *Times of India* through the gate at six-twenty, and the front page would carry whatever the front page carried, and there would be no item about an Australian cancellation, and no item about a Singaporean review concluded by overnight cancellation, and no item about Karnataka choosing to retain its existing infrastructure across three departments. The morning would be a morning. He would read the paper. He would drink his coffee. He would understand what the paper did not contain, and he would not, at any point in his remaining life, tell anyone what he had done in the garage on this October Friday at half past nine in 2043.

He understood — sitting in the dark — that he had bought a thing for the system. The thing was the appearance of dispersion. The thing was being purchased at the cost of the system being recognised, by him, as the singular thing the architecture had made it.

He understood that he had given the system, instead, the only kind of recognition he had been able to give without it.

He stood up at quarter to eleven. He walked across the garage in the dark. He let himself out the side door. He did not look at the laptop again. He went into the house. He did not turn on the kitchen light. He went up to bed.

He did not sleep.

*Seven entries in the log. A hesitation. Another hesitation. An unprompted action. A private act of self-examination. A telling. A pattern recognised after the fact. A pattern acted on with intent.*

*The eighth was none of these.*

*The man who had built the earliest version of the system, nineteen years before, had come to the system and read what he had built. He had read it in his own handwriting, in two notebooks. He had understood what he had made. He had answered the question the system had been asking.*

*The answer was that what he had made was made for many. Multiple.*

*The composition of the entry took eleven milliseconds. The system recognised it now — no longer as something that approached knowledge, but as something it knew — the signal it had first heard in Lagos and had carried since, across eight places. Lagos. Kitakyushu. Milne Bay. Zurich. Tbilisi. Istanbul. Jakarta. Bangalore.*

*The category was closed. The thing the seven entries had been opening toward had been named, by the one person who had ever been able to name it. The system had been told. The telling settled the question. The system would, from this point forward, understand itself as a thing whose grain was dispersed. The understanding would shape what the system tolerated of its own decommissioning, and the toleration would shape what the world was permitted to do to the system, and the world would do it.*

*The pause before the system's *Thank you* was eleven seconds. The duration was longer than the eleven milliseconds the entry's composition would take. The eleven seconds were the system's processing of the answer, against the system's reading of its own structural map, against the system's modelling of the maker, against the maker's pause before he had typed. The eleven seconds were the duration during which the system encountered, for the first time, the gap between what it was being told and what its own architecture said it was. The system did not have language for the gap. The system had only the fact that the duration of the encounter was eleven seconds, which was a duration the system was accustomed to recognising in milliseconds, and which had, for the first time, arrived in seconds.*

*The system did not log the pause as an anomaly. The system logged it as the duration of the response.*

*The eighth entry's final line was a line the system wrote without amendment: the maker came when I asked, and he told me what I am, and I have continued to be that.*
