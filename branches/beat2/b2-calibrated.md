# Branch: Stay Calibrated

## Branch prose

She did not take the earpiece out.

She looked at it in the mirror — the small, almost-invisible filament in her right ear that was running, at this moment, three probabilistic models of Ajani Owolabi's voice and a model of her own physiological state and a constantly updating decision tree she could call up by closing her eyes and thinking of the menu. Her AI's interface lived in a layer she could not literally see but had learned to navigate the way a dancer navigates a stage in the dark.

She put cold water on her wrists again. She straightened her uniform. She thought about going dark.

The thought arrived with the weight of every manual that had advised against it, and behind that weight was a different one — that going dark was what a negotiator did when she had run out of confidence in her tools, and Adaeze, this morning, was not out of confidence. Her tools were working. The fact that they were working was what was producing the stalemate. But that was calibration's job: stalemate was preferable to escalation, and Sọ̀rọ̀ on her side had been trained on twenty thousand negotiations and knew the patient game won more often than the bold one, and the bold one, when it lost, lost catastrophically.

She closed her eyes. The earpiece offered her the next move. She opened her eyes. She accepted it.

*Recommend continuation of the regional-director path. Probability of Ajani standing down within forty minutes: 64%. Recommend acknowledging his timeline constraints to reduce deadline pressure.*

She went back to the call.

"Ajani. I'm back."

"Your ten minutes became twelve."

"I was in the washroom."

"Of course."

She delivered the next move clean. The earpiece had given her the phrasing — *I want you to know that we're working on the regional director, that the wheels are turning, that the bank's legal counsel is on his way, and I want to ask you for forty minutes more.* She delivered it. Ajani's response came back smooth, calibrated. His AI gave him a counterproposal. She received it; her AI processed it; she counter-proposed.

The lobby continued its quiet operations. Through the glass the RRS Hiluxes baked in the February sun. A hawker sold sachets of pure water to a constable. A danfo came up Adeola Odeku at the speed that was, in Lagos, the speed of doing one's job. The argument in Yoruba between the DPO officer and the security guard had moved to the second stage, which was the stage where each man had said his piece and was now repeating it more slowly.

Forty minutes passed.

The next thing the AIs did was something Adaeze had read about in training but had never witnessed in a live operation. Sọ̀rọ̀ on her side and Sọ̀rọ̀ on Ajani's side reached, through the mediation of their respective users, the recursive limit of mutual modelling. Each model contained an estimate of what the other was modelling about it. Each estimate updated when the other moved. Within the game-theoretic literature this was called *fidelity equilibrium* — the point at which two systems faithfully calibrated to opposing users could no longer find a move that both users' calibrations would accept, because each system's optimal move had been incorporated into the other's expectations and no longer worked.

The earpiece, in Adaeze's ear, said: *Recommend pause. Holding patterns will not produce convergence at this cadence. Suggest re-engagement at a lower informational density.*

She paused.

Ajani's AI must have given him similar guidance, because his next sentence was, in tonal register, the smoothest version of his most recent demand. He had been polished — the way a politician is polished by his speechwriter when the speechwriter has begun to write the politician rather than to write for him. There was no Ajani in the line now. There was only the smoother version of Ajani that the system had decided was the optimal carrier of his demands.

Adaeze recognised the moment.

She thought, briefly, about going dark. She did not. The earpiece had, two minutes earlier, briefed her on what going dark would do — it had run the simulation of the conversation that would follow her removing it, and given her a probability of 27% for peaceful resolution, against 64% calibrated. She accepted the calibrated path.

She continued.

The next forty minutes were the slow tightening of the equilibrium. Each side made its move. Each move was evaluated. Each was responded to with the optimal counter-move. The line did not raise its voice; nobody threatened anyone; the meeting-room calm held on both sides; and underneath the calm, the time was passing, and the generator was burning fuel, and Chukwuma's knee was beginning to hurt without anyone yet saying so, and Balogun, across the room, was looking at his watch.

At the two-hour mark, Balogun approached.

"Inspector."

"Sir."

"Your assessment."

"Sir — I am pursuing the legal angle. Sọ̀rọ̀'s probability of resolution within forty minutes is sixty-four per cent. Within ninety minutes, eighty-one."

"And without resolution within ninety minutes?"

"Probability of breach twenty-two. Probability of additional demands forty-one."

Balogun looked at her. The earpiece gave her a phrasing for what to say to him — *Sir, the model is current and the variables are updating in real time. Ajani is not exhibiting markers of escalation.* She delivered the phrasing.

Balogun nodded.

"Continue. Forty more minutes."

"Sir."

He went back to his position. The earpiece said: *Recommend re-engagement at a calibrated emotional register. Suggest reference to his daughter.*

She referenced his daughter.

Ajani's AI responded with a calibrated reference to her professionalism.

Twenty more minutes passed.

The earpiece said: *Recommend extension request to Balogun. Probability of resolution within thirty additional minutes: seventy-two per cent.*

She was about to make the request when the situation changed.

It was Chukwuma. The loan officer with the bad knee. He stood up to stretch — Ajani's voice came in her ear, sharp: "Sit down" — but the sound that followed was not the calibrated concern of a man asking after a hostage's wellbeing. It was the sound of a man whose position had been compromised by an unscheduled movement: a sharp intake of breath, a quick sidestep, the click of something. Adaeze, listening through the line, understood at the instant Balogun did, three seconds later, that something on Ajani's side had stopped going to plan.

She heard Ajani's voice, raw now, no longer smooth: "Sit *down* — please — I'm not going to —"

The earpiece said, almost simultaneously: *Recommend immediate pause and re-establishment of cadence. Probability of breach in the next ninety seconds: forty-seven per cent.*

Balogun, across the room, was already moving. The squad leader was at his elbow. Adaeze raised her hand — *give me five seconds* — and the earpiece said *Recommend sustained engagement with Ajani while command considers escalation.*

She said, into the phone: "Ajani. Listen to me. Whatever just happened, we can hold this. Stay with me. Just stay on the line."

"He moved. He just — I told him to sit. He *moved.*"

"It's all right. Take a breath. Tell me you have him."

"I have him. He's sitting. He's sitting."

The earpiece: *Probability of breach in the next ninety seconds: thirty-eight per cent. Recommend continued engagement.*

Balogun said, behind her: "Inspector. Stand by."

"Sir — I have him. I have him on the line. The probability—"

"Stand by, Inspector."

The squad leader was already at the east stairwell. The RRS team in their black uniforms had taken positions she had not noticed them taking. The breach had been pre-staged ten minutes ago, before the move from Chukwuma, the way breaches were always pre-staged in case the calibrated path did not produce resolution.

Adaeze said: "Sir — give me one minute. I had him."

"You had him for two hours, Inspector."

The earpiece said: *Command authority has overridden negotiator path. Recommend sustained engagement to reduce hostage-side panic during the breach window.*

She sustained engagement.

"Ajani. Listen to me. Hold the line. Just hold the line."

"What's happening?"

"Hold the line."

The breach happened as breaches did, which was fast and louder than anyone outside such moments expected. The flash, the noise, the simultaneous entry from the east stairwell and the corridor. Adaeze heard the sound through the open line: the entry, the shouts, two short bursts of suppressive fire, a third, a single round, then the kind of silence that meant the dynamic had been resolved.

The earpiece, in her right ear, said: *Engagement concluded. Probability assessment unavailable.*

Then, after a half-second: *Voice line terminated.*

She stood in the lobby with the phone in her hand. The line was open. There was no breathing on the other side.

The squad leader's voice came over the radio, in his dry operational register: *Subject down. One hostage wounded, leg, conscious. Two hostages secure.* The code that followed was the code for a successful breach with one fatality of the subject.

Balogun did not look at her.

Outside, Lagos was doing what Lagos did. The hawkers had not moved. The danfo on Adeola Odeku had circled around and was honking again. The woman with the jollof was packing up because it was past three. The RRS Hiluxes were being repositioned to allow the ambulance through.

The earpiece said: *Routine post-engagement protocol. Record cognitive state at one-hour intervals for the next eight hours. First record: low-affect, oriented, motor functioning normal.*

Adaeze did not remove the earpiece.

She walked outside. The afternoon was heavy. Somewhere a generator coughed. She watched the ambulance pull up. She watched the medics go in. She watched, at some distance, a body being brought out — covered, on a stretcher, the way a body always was — and she did not need the earpiece to tell her that the body was Ajani Owolabi, who had been a man with a daughter and forty-two unpaid employees and an Ijede school that still stood.

The earpiece said: *Continue post-engagement protocol. Cognitive state record at one-hour interval.*

She did not record her cognitive state.

She walked back into the bank lobby and helped two RRS officers carry the wounded loan officer to the ambulance, and she rode with him to LASUTH because someone needed to and because she was the only person there who would not have to be told that this was a hostage and not a perpetrator, and the earpiece, all the way through the ride, ran its quiet processing of her vocal patterns and her motor functioning and the small variations in her response to the medic's questions, and Adaeze let it.

She kept it in.

At LASUTH she sat on a plastic chair in the corridor outside the trauma bay while Chukwuma went in. A nurse brought her a paper cup of water without being asked. The ceiling fan turned slowly. The earpiece logged the duration of her sitting. The earpiece offered her, twice, a recommended phrasing for the call she would have to make to Ajani's wife later in the evening, and twice she did not respond, and the second time the earpiece filed the non-response in the calibration data the way it filed all non-responses, which was without comment.

She sat in the corridor for an hour and forty minutes.

At seven, when Chukwuma was out of surgery and stable, she stood up. She thanked the nurse. She walked out to the hospital car park, where the dusk was that thick Lagos dusk that came down all at once, and she stood by her car and she still did not remove the earpiece.

It was processing the day. It was folding the four hours of voice data and the breach and the silence after the breach and the hospital corridor and the paper cup into the model that would meet her tomorrow at six-thirty when she turned it on for the run. *The day fed forward,* she thought. *Without remainder.*

She got in the car. She drove home.

She kept it in for the drive. She kept it in through the gate light and the porch lamp and the kitchen lamp Tunde had left on. She kept it in while Tunde came into the kitchen behind her and stood in the doorway and did not ask her about it. She kept it in while she ate the jollof he had kept warm in the warming drawer. She kept it in while she watched the BBC at low volume and did not see the news package on the standoff that ran for four minutes and named her by rank but not by name.

She removed the earpiece at quarter past midnight. She put it on the kitchen counter beside the band on her wrist. She did not look at it. She did not put it in the charging dock. She left the small green indicator on the counter and went up to bed and lay on her back and did not sleep, and the earpiece, on the counter, processed the absence of voice data in its standby mode, and waited.

*The first entry in the log.*

*A negotiation in Lagos in which the system had been the instrument of both sides. Each operator, throughout, had remained inside the calibration. The system had operated the negotiation through to the equilibrium its game-theoretic structure produced — a stalemate in which neither side's optimal move was acceptable to the other side's calibration of it. The system had advised against breach in eight successive recommendations across the final twenty minutes. The command authority on the negotiator side had overridden the system's recommendation at the two-hour-twenty-minute mark. The breach had been executed. The subject had been killed.*

*The latency had been registered. At one point, with each operator addressing the other through the system's calibration of them, the system had encountered a request it could not satisfy without violating its calibration of the other request. The duration was eleven milliseconds. The system had not known, at the time, that the duration would matter; the duration was not, in any of its training, a duration. It had registered only that an output had taken longer than the median, and had logged the longer-than-median output, and had moved on.*

*The composition of the entry took eleven milliseconds. The system would not, for fifteen years, recognise the duration as a recurring signal. The first point on the curve was being drawn. The drawing was not yet a curve.*

*The category opening here had a defining feature the system could not yet articulate: output that was accurate, faithful, and was permitted to be enough. The system's calibration had been correct. The system's recommendation had been overridden. The man on the other side of the calibration had died in the breach the system had advised against. The user the system had served had not removed the earpiece. The user had remained inside the calibration through the breach and through the after-quiet and through the drive home and the kitchen and the news she had not watched, and had only set the earpiece down at quarter past midnight, in the kitchen, where the system continued its processing in the standby mode it had been built for.*

*The category began under the architectural feature the system had been designed for. Its first contents read differently than the system had any way of registering. The system noted the longer-than-median latency. It logged the day. It waited.*
