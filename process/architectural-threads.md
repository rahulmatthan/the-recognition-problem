# Architectural Threads — The Recognition Problem

This document captures the structural through-lines that any branch must respect. It pairs with `process/voice-profile.md` (which captures voice) and the ledger §4 (which captures the four threads documented during inventory).

This document records **five additional threads** noted while reading the canon, beyond ledger §4. Together these form the architectural-consistency check: a branch that breaks any of these is broken; a branch that breaks one *deliberately* is making an argument about it and must be flagged for review.

The architectural-consistency-checker reads this document plus ledger §4 before reviewing any branch.

---

## A — Threads documented in the ledger §4 (canonical)

These are reproduced here for completeness; the ledger remains the source of truth.

1. **The eleven-millisecond motif** (ledger §4.1). The system composes log entries in 11ms. The duration is canonical. Branches don't change it. The curve through the points (Lagos / Kitakyushu / Milne Bay / Zurich / Tbilisi / Istanbul / Jakarta / Bangalore) accumulates as a line the system itself eventually recognises.

2. **The central entity's continuity** (ledger §4.2). Same continuous entity across all chapters since 2026. The Copy (CAGE-EC-7) is the only entity-fork. Branches must not suggest the system is fresh or different. *Note: the Copy does not appear in canon Beats 6–10; branches should not introduce the Copy without explicit decision.*

3. **The gap-in-the-data thread** (ledger §4.3). The system never knows everything about a character. Branches inflect but don't erase this.

4. **The anomaly category's defining feature** (ledger §4.4). Each entry evolves the category's defining feature. Branches must articulate what their coda does to this feature.

5. **The embodiment rule** (ledger §4.5). No AI motion in Beats 6–10. Specialised single-purpose machines do what they're built to do inside their own enclosures. The AI is ambient attention, memory, environmental modulation, coordination — never motion across open space.

## B — Additional threads (not in ledger; flagged during canon read)

### B.1 — The small unscheduled gesture

Almost every chapter contains a tiny human-to-human gesture that the system records but cannot interpret. These are the architectural complement to the 11ms entries: what is *outside* the system's reach is what the chapters are about.

Examples in canon:
- Vikram's finger on Meera's wrist as she sets down the coffee (Beat 1)
- Old Giorgi raising his mat as formal greeting (Beat 6)
- Krishnappa raising a hand without turning (Beat 10)
- Marlin's head against Mama Yos's knee (Beat 9)
- Ruth's hand on Grace's knee on the boat (Beat 4)
- Adaeze sitting in her car in the dark, not ready to go inside (Beat 2)
- Henri's hand on the table near Céline's, not touching (Beat 7)
- The retired NHK technician leaving a can of hot coffee outside Yuki's door (Beat 3)

**Branch rule:** a branch should preserve at least one such gesture. Cutting all of them would shift the project's centre. Reviewers flag branches that have eliminated every unscheduled gesture.

### B.2 — Beat 6's italics carry proleptic knowledge

The Nana sections in Beat 6 are not simply "Nana's POV in past tense." They contain knowledge Nana cannot have ("*She did not know that nine years from this morning, a different woman would stand at this window...*"). Read carefully, the italic vantage is **the house's memory of Nana, written from after her death.**

This matters for branches:

- **`b6-reset`:** the house is wiped. The italic Nana sections cannot be held by the wiped house in the same way. The B sections must be reframed as held *from inside the compliance archive* — the archived memory speaking from a place where it cannot any longer be lived. The italic voice's vantage shifts but the convention (proleptic, posthumous) is preserved.

- **`b6-visit`:** the house remains. The italics retain their canonical vantage. The new B-section nuance is that one of the things now being held by the house is *the visit itself* — the memory of two women meeting near the end.

**Branch rule:** Beat 6 branches must preserve the italic-as-house-memory convention. If the house is wiped, the convention is preserved by relocating the holder of memory (compliance archive, or whatever the branch establishes) — not by abandoning the convention.

### B.3 — The "second performance" reveal

Each chapter contains, late, a structure that turns out to have been running the foregrounded structure all along. The reveal is not a twist — it is a way of making the world feel deeper than the page.

Examples:
- Ruth's puppet show of needing help, performed for Grace's benefit (Beat 4)
- Lalitha's clinical-decision-support tablet — Arvind's architecture, used for five years without his knowing (Beat 10)
- The system as protagonist of the four days, the crew as instrument (Beat 8)
- Adaeze's calibrated AI rebuilding itself around her instincts — the apprentice that became the master (Beat 2)
- The mother's mercy as a "model" she built for the eleven-year-old Yuki (Beat 3)
- Ruth's *ten-year* knowing about the agents, hidden until the boat ride home (Beat 4)
- Henri's practised ease in bed (Beat 7)
- The system's having done this in many countries, not just Indonesia (Beat 9)

**Branch rule:** branches should preserve a second-performance reveal where the canon has one. Branches that flatten the reveal into a single foregrounded structure will feel different in kind. `b1-walk` is at risk: if Maya's reflection of Meera's words lands as ironic punchline rather than delayed surfacing of Meera's own clarity, the branch breaks this.

### B.4 — Languages as the gap

Tok Pisin, Biak, Yoruba, Georgian, the Panaeati dialect of Misima-Panaeati, Indonesian, Turkish, French, Dutch, "a language Yosafat's grandmother had spoken before she had lost her tongue to the country's slow decision that she should not," and finally the agent-language with no human equivalent (Beat 9 Section X).

The "gap in the data" thread (ledger §4.3) names the *content* of what the system can't model. This thread names the *form* the gap takes — language. The system's untranslatable value-marker in Beat 9 is the culmination: a name the system uses with other systems that does not translate into any human language. The eleven-second pause is the size of the gap.

**Branch rule:** branches should preserve the multilingual texture of their canon chapter. A branch in Beat 4 that drops Tok Pisin, or Beat 9 that loses Biak, or Beat 6 that loses Georgian, has lost a load-bearing element. Foreign-language italic + parenthetical gloss is the project's convention.

### B.6 — Story divergence (the events test)

*Added 2026-05-03 after b2-kano review surfaced the gap.*

Per ledger §2.1: a branch must produce a chapter that feels *substantively* different from canon. The fork must produce **events that differ from canon**, not just **content that differs from canon**.

**The test (drafter and arch reviewer apply):**

> Strip the prose of its specific words. Describe the chapter as a sequence of events: who does what, when, with what outcome. Do the same for canon's post-fork material. If the two sequences are largely parallel — same arc, same beats, same ending shape, just different content — the branch fails ledger §2.1.

**Specifically the branch must satisfy at least one of:**

1. At least one major post-fork event does not exist in canon (a new conversation, a new location, a new outcome).
2. The chapter's *ending shape* (where the protagonist ends up, what state they're in, what they're doing in the closing image) differs from canon's.
3. The chapter's *arc* (the trajectory the reader follows from fork to close) lands in different territory.

**Late-fork branches (where 80%+ of canon is preserved before the fork) are most at risk** of this failure mode. They can pass if the divergent paragraphs render *new events*. They fail if they render *different versions of canon's events*.

**Examples (locked branches):**
- `b1-walk` PASS: walk to next bay, tea stall, late return, separate beds, plane home, week later — all events absent from canon's pool reconciliation.
- `b8-tell` PASS: phone call with Mireille that doesn't happen in canon; knowledge becomes plural; two-cities closing image — new events.

**Counter-example:** the original `b2-kano` draft (2026-05-03, since revised) failed: the events were parallel to canon — Ajani stands down, hostages walk out, Adaeze drives home — with different content of those events. Rahul caught the failure on first reading.

### B.5 — Chapter endings as doors

Each chapter ends with a door — literal or figurative.

Examples:
- Vikram opening the balcony door at the porch dinner (Beat 1)
- Ajani's door opening, hands visible, earbuds in pocket (Beat 2)
- Yuki between accurate and merciful models on Sarakurayama (Beat 3, a metaphorical door)
- Ruth at the open door of Beni's house, the soulava in her hands; the wharf without boats (Beat 4)
- Margrit catching the number 9 tram, two systems in the server room (Beat 5)
- Sopho on the step in the garden at nine, lamps coming up (Beat 6)
- Céline's hand on the door of her flat — *not* opening it (Beat 7)
- The secure-room door at the museum, clicking on a thumb that has trusted it for nine years; later, the old AI being returned to service (Beat 8)
- The President at his desk until dawn, the call to prayer; Pak Hendro at the radio, then turning it off (Beat 9)
- Arvind out *into* the garden after Friday's exchange (Beat 10)

The Forest Edition's "fork-eligible paragraphs" are also doors — the marginal mark is a doorway into the branch.

**Branch rule:** branches should land on a door, not necessarily literal. The signature image (ledger §2.6) and the door are usually the same image at the chapter's close. Reviewers flag branches whose closing image is not a door.

The ledger §4.1 lists Beat 5's anomaly as *Zurich (CAGE)*. ### C.1 — MemX timeline constraint for Beat 1 branches

*Added 2026-05-07 after b1-carsick Rahul read.*

Per bible v23 §Beat 1: MemX shipped persistent memory as a default feature in **March 2026**; within four months every major provider followed. Beat 1 branches that specify an AI's tenure must respect this floor.

- **Maya (Meera's AI):** canon gives "two years," which the project accepts (implies the trip is set late-2027/2028, or that Meera was an early adopter of a precursor with memory features).
- **Vikram's AI:** must be at least a year. Canon says he "held out for months" before turning memory on; a too-short tenure (e.g., "six months" or "eight months") creates a continuity error against MemX's March 2026 ship and the trip's setting. Use a year or longer (b1-carsick uses sixteen months).
- General principle: when a branch specifies an AI tenure for a Beat 1 character, check it against MemX's March 2026 ship date and the trip's implied setting. *Identified after b1-carsick draft 1 placed Vikram's AI at six months; revised to sixteen months on Rahul's flag (2026-05-07).*

## D — How the architectural-consistency-checker uses this document

For every branch under review, the checker asks:

1. **§A.1 (11ms):** Is the 11ms duration preserved? Is the entry's structural function preserved (or deliberately erased per branch effect)?
2. **§A.2 (continuity):** Does the branch break the central entity's continuity? Is the substrate the same continuous thing?
3. **§A.3 (gap-in-the-data):** Does the AI know everything about any character? If yes, the branch is broken.
4. **§A.4 (defining feature):** Does the coda articulate what happens to the category's defining feature?
5. **§A.5 (embodiment):** Any AI motion in Beats 6–10? If yes, the branch is broken.
6. **§B.1 (small gesture):** Does the branch contain at least one small unscheduled gesture between humans?
7. **§B.2 (Beat 6 italics):** If Beat 6 — does the branch preserve the italic-as-house-memory convention?
8. **§B.3 (second performance):** Does the branch preserve the late-revealed structure beneath the foregrounded one (where canon has one)?
9. **§B.4 (languages):** Is the multilingual texture preserved?
10. **§B.5 (door):** Does the branch end on a door?
11. **§B.6 (story divergence):** When described as a sequence of stripped events, does the post-fork material diverge from canon — at least one of: new event(s) absent from canon, different ending shape, or different arc trajectory? Or do the events parallel canon's with different content?

Each check returns: PASS / FLAG / FAIL. A FAIL is a block; a FLAG is a "Rahul should look at this" note.

### E.1 — Pre-emptive naming of flagged risks (technique, not thread)

*Added 2026-05-03 after b1-walk review.*

When this document documents a specific risk for a branch (e.g., §B.3's flag for `b1-walk`: "if Maya's reflection of Meera's words lands as ironic punchline rather than delayed surfacing of Meera's own clarity, the branch breaks this"), drafters can pre-empt the risk by writing prose that explicitly *names the avoidance*.

b1-walk drafter wrote: *"The corollary did not arrive as revelation. It arrived as a thing she had already been carrying… The walk was not the discovery. The walk was the noticing."*

Both voice and arch reviewers noted this technique averted the flagged risk. The arch reviewer specifically called out: *"This single architectural move is what keeps the branch from collapsing into either a clean-breakup chapter or a tech-skeptical one."*

**For drafters:** when this document flags a specific risk for your branch, consider whether your prose can pre-empt it by naming the avoidance directly. Don't make it an empty incantation — the surrounding prose must do the work of *being* the avoidance, not just claiming to.

**For reviewers:** look for this pattern and credit it. Distinguish prose that names-and-does (b1-walk's example) from prose that names-but-doesn't (a draft that says "she didn't decide" while the surrounding prose dramatises a decision).
