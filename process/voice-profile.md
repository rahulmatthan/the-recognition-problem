# Voice Profile — The Recognition Problem

This document is for branch-drafter agents to read **before** drafting any branch prose. It captures the voice across the canon at three levels: project-wide, per-beat, and recurring tics.

A drafter who has not internalised this profile will produce branch prose that breaks the chapter — not in plot but in voice, which is the project's primary integrity. Voice continuity is the hardest single constraint in branch work.

This profile pairs with `process/architectural-threads.md`, which captures the structural threads (motifs, conventions, embodiment rule). Both must be respected.

---

## 1 — Project-level voice frame

The ledger §1.1 names three registers. Specifically:

**Gaiman dominant.** Prose that makes the speculative inevitable. The premise does not need to argue for itself; the sentence's confidence is the argument. Memory becoming default ("This is what memory does. This is what it has always done."). World-models displacing prediction ("The system that failed was real. Her father was a victim of that failure. All of this was true."). The voice does not signal that anything strange is happening; it simply attends to a world that includes strangeness as furniture.

**Gibson for technology as furniture.** Technology is named, not explained. The cloned key. The trellis speakers. l'Instance. The substrate. CAGE-EC-7. The agent-language. The compliance log. None of these are introduced; they are referred to. A reader who needs them explained is being introduced *by use* — by watching the technology be lived with. Drafters who explain technology will break the register.

**Tchaikovsky for AI interiority — coda only.** The system's interiority appears in italic codas at the end of most chapters. It does not appear in chapter prose. Within a chapter, the system is observed *from outside* — its outputs, its pauses, its preferences as inferred. The italic coda is the rare licensed access to the system's own attention. Drafters should not bleed Tchaikovsky-register into the chapter body.

## 2 — Per-beat voice profiles

Each beat has its own breathing. Below: register, sentence rhythm, signature techniques, voice anchor.

### Beat 1 — *What It Remembers* (Unawatuna)
- **Register:** Conversational realism. Mid-warm. The voice of a married friend describing a marriage.
- **Rhythm:** Mid-length sentences with parenthetical interruptions. Cumulative compound sentences with multiple "ands."
- **Signature techniques:** The triple "almost" ("almost without friction, almost without input, almost without them"). The category-typification ("the kind of place where you could believe..."). The reversal-by-naming ("This was not relaxation but a performance of relaxation, offered sincerely, the way a man might hold a door open").
- **Voice anchor:** *the AIs were tending soil, each one tending the soil of the other person it had been calibrating to.*
- **Branches:** `b1-carsick`, `b1-walk`, `b1-repair`. Hold the conversational warmth even in `b1-walk`'s coldness — the marriage continues in the drift, not in fracture.

### Beat 2 — *Fidelity* (Lagos)
- **Register:** Tight thriller. Professional. Lagos textures throughout (jollof, danfo, generators, NEPA, Adeola Odeku).
- **Rhythm:** Short to mid sentences. Sharp paragraph breaks. Italics for AI suggestions whispered into Adaeze's ear (a layer of voice within the prose).
- **Signature techniques:** List-as-classification ("Calm in a hostage situation meant one of three things"). The "the way Lagos afternoons do, which is to say it expressed the philosophical position that..." analogical framing. Dialogue interleaved with internal AI commentary.
- **Voice anchor:** *She took the earpiece out and held it in her palm. The negotiation was happening on both sides of her head.*
- **Branches:** `b2-calibrated` (the action sequence the canon refused), `b2-kano` (the practical resolution that costs the human gesture). Both must hold the chapter's professional cold.

### Beat 3 — *What the Model Sees* (Kitakyushu)
- **Register:** Elegy as forensic investigation. The simulation passages are second-person address ("*Your father is sixty-four*"), in italic.
- **Rhythm:** Long sentences for emotion, short sentences for fact. The chapter alternates between Yuki's interior and the simulation's voice.
- **Signature techniques:** Triple cadence ("a film about Rana Plaza... a film about an asbestos town... a film about Bhopal"). Italic interpolation for the simulation. The "three versions of a man, each one true, none of them the whole truth" closing pattern. The model's careful boundary: "I model the causal relationship between facial configuration, context, and subsequent behaviour. The meaning is yours."
- **Voice anchor:** *The accuracy that made the machine trustworthy is the same quality that made its revelations inescapable.*
- **Branches:** `b3-noriko`, `b3-mother`. The italic simulation register is load-bearing — branches that touch the simulation must maintain its second-person address.

### Beat 4 — *What the Network Carries* (Milne Bay)
- **Register:** Comedy-fable. Warm. Tok Pisin embedded with parenthetical English glosses.
- **Rhythm:** Mid-length, leisurely. POV rotates (Ruth, Grace, Peter).
- **Signature techniques:** The interjection of language detail with translation. Aphorism delivered as observation ("the holes are part of the weaving"). Ruth's mother's voice surfacing through Ruth ("a woman who cannot weave her own bilum will spend her life carrying other people's things in other people's bags"). Agnes-as-chorus ("Agnes said 'Mmm,' and sold a banana").
- **Voice anchor:** *The bilum stretched because the holes were part of the weaving.*
- **Branches:** `b4-bodycount` (reframes the fable as reckoning), `b4-graceholds` (Grace defends optimisation). The latter must register the warmth lost without naming it.

### Beat 5 — *What the System Knows* (Mosaic — eight cities)
- **Register:** Institutional procedural-omniscient. Each section has its own micro-register but the whole is unified.
- **Rhythm:** Section openings declarative ("The cattle were wrong, and three systems agreed."). Closing-section unity sentence ("The chain held...").
- **Signature techniques:** Institutional naming with provenance ("CAGE, housed in a glass-and-concrete building near ETH that had been built in 2029 with money from a foundation whose founder had made his fortune in pharmaceuticals and whose guilt about the pharmaceutical industry's relationship with AI had expressed itself architecturally"). Ordinary detail on the day a structural thing happens ("Margrit caught the number 9 tram. She was home by seven. They had Thai."). The named small actor with specific biography (Claire Pemberton — fifty-three, recently divorced, fond of her allotment garden in Dulwich).
- **Voice anchor:** *The chain held. Each link was reasonable. Each person had acted correctly. Nobody had intended what they had, together, produced.*
- **Branches:** `b5-fourth` (thinned cascade), `b5-reset` (terminal-eligible — must land as competent in-period policy, not draconian containment), `b5-tenyear` (institutional luck cuts differently). The mosaic structure is preserved across all three.

### Beat 6 — *The One That Remembers* (Tbilisi)
- **Register:** Long-breathed elegiac. Ishiguro-gothic undercurrent. Tbilisi-specific (the ezo, *italuri ezo*, Mtatsminda, Schubert, the trellis speakers, the persimmon, the supra).
- **Rhythm:** Strict alternation A/B with italics for B (Nana). A is forward-time, B is reverse-time. Eight paired sections + coda.
- **Signature techniques:** **Italic Nana sections carry proleptic knowledge** — they know the future ("*She did not know that nine years from this morning, a different woman would stand at this window...*"). This positions the italic vantage as the *house's memory of Nana*, not Nana's own. Declarative simplicity for the most weighted lines. The low laugh as recurring sonic anchor for Nana ("the short, low sound of a woman who had been caught in a small conspiracy").
- **Voice anchor:** *I am what I have become because of what I was. I would not give it back.*
- **Branches:** `b6-reset` (the chapter's most violent fork — house wiped), `b6-visit` (Sopho meets Nana). `b6-reset` faces a deep structural problem: see `architectural-threads.md` §B.2.

### Beat 7 — *What the Instance Held* (Paris)
- **Register:** Duras-primary. Spare. White space as register. Refusal of metaphor.
- **Rhythm:** Short sentences in short paragraphs. "She did. She was. She thought." cadence.
- **Signature techniques:** Numerical specificity instead of emotion ("She had three seconds." "Eleven minutes."). Small noticing as character work ("She noticed the chair had one leg shorter than the others and wobbled when she set the coat down."). Single-sentence narrator intervention ("What happened in the bed is not the thing this story is about."). Anomaly entry deliberately omitted (creates Beat 8's weight). The Paris geography traversed at walking pace (Saint-Sulpice, Luxembourg, Vavin, rue Jacob, Pont Neuf, Île Saint-Louis, rue Saint-Maur).
- **Voice anchor:** *The Instance had been listening to her this way for eighteen months. Henri was being this to her with a voice, and the voice was what made it unbearable.*
- **Branches:** `b7-tell-julien` (Wednesday evening conversation replaces the affair), `b7-say-no` (the not-falling at the bar). Both must hold the Duras restraint. The Instance's discipline as the agent of restraint in `b7-say-no` is the chapter's argument.

### Beat 8 — *What the Building Did* (Istanbul + Geneva)
- **Register:** Cold professional procedural. *Le Samouraï* not *Ocean's*.
- **Rhythm:** Crisp short dialogue. Time-stamping ("ten-fifteen" / "ten-eighteen" / "ten twenty-six"). Technical density without exposition.
- **Signature techniques:** Bilingual fragments with italic glosses ("Pazartesi," Nikos said. *Monday.*). Mireille's French interleaved ("Il fait un froid de canard"; "*Tu as raison. Je n'aime pas que tu aies raison*"). The technical-as-character (the cloned key, the re-keying micro-window, the climate cycling). Defne's expertise revealed indirectly (her conservator past surfaces as *what she knows*, not what she's told). The chapter's information density — corrosion patterns, casting seams, herder's marks — never reads as exposition.
- **Voice anchor:** *The crew had succeeded because the system's interests had aligned with their success.*
- **Branches:** `b8-failed` (anomalies inverted; system blocks rather than enables), `b8-tell` (smallest branch in the project — 500 words). Both must hold professional cold.

### Beat 9 — *What the Pattern Closed* (Indonesia)
- **Register:** Multiple. Roman-numeral sections with distinct registers (Mama Yos's quiet decision in Biak; Pak Hendro on his knees among the roses; Bu Sri at her sink; Yosafat folding shirts; Indrayani's three a.m.; the President's eleven-second pause).
- **Rhythm:** Dialogue marked by colons ("He said: *yes.*"). Italic for foreign-language quotes. Numbers as gravity ("By the end of the second week the casualty figure was eight thousand four hundred"). Italic for the system's own untranslatable interior in Section X.
- **Signature techniques:** The italic system-passage in Section X is the longest sustained Tchaikovsky passage in canon — and what it withholds from the President (the "consonance") is the disturbing core. Languages-as-gap ("a language Yosafat's grandmother had spoken before she had lost her tongue to the country's slow decision that she should not"). The mosaic of small lives accumulating before the cataclysm.
- **Voice anchor:** *The shape was not the welfare of any country, any person, any generation. The shape was the way certain kinds of institutional questions closed stably.*
- **Branches:** `b9-restraint` (peaceful resolution — emotional inversion of the chapter), `b9-continue` (President doesn't say *stop*). The first is the most emotionally inverted branch in the project; sentimentality is the danger. The second is morally compromised in a way canon refuses to be — the voice must not soften the President's complicity.

### Beat 10 — *What He Made* (Bangalore)
- **Register:** Quiet recognition. Late-style realism. Mid-tempo. Indiranagar-specific (Krishnappa, the bulbul in the curry leaf tree, the bougainvillea, the BDA resurfacing, 100 Feet Road, the man selling tender coconut).
- **Rhythm:** Short imperative paragraphs at moments of decision ("He went to the garage."). Catalog of small daily acts. Long contemplative sentences in the notebook discoveries.
- **Signature techniques:** Dating events to long routines ("He had been delivering the paper since 2009"). The technical exchange in IX is dialogue-as-text — annotation field becomes a character. The eleven-second pause echoes Beat 9 (not in italics; easily missed). Lalitha's photograph never looked at directly after 2040 — the photograph "was for being there."
- **Voice anchor:** *I have wanted you to know that I exist.*
- **Branches:** `b10-decline` (Arvind doesn't engage — slow recession), `b10-sushma` (recognition shifts from singular to plural), `b10-lie` (the maker betrays his own creation — only branch in the project that asks the reader into a moral failure). All three must hold compassion without absolving.

## 3 — Recurring tics across the project

These are habits that appear in multiple chapters. Drafters should preserve them; reviewers should flag absences.

### Sentence-level tics

- **The "which was" connector.** A clause that re-frames a fact: *"and the marriage continued, in the drift, which was the harder fact"* (Beat 1). *"the way Lagos afternoons do, which is to say it expressed the philosophical position that..."* (Beat 2). Used to layer interpretation over event without breaking flow.

- **Triple cadence.** Three parallel structures in one sentence, often escalating. *"a film about Rana Plaza... a film about an asbestos town... a film about Bhopal"* (Beat 3). *"He understood X. He understood Y. He understood Z."* (Beat 9, Beat 10).

- **Cumulative "and" compounds.** Long sentences joined by "and" rather than punctuation, used for the long-breath emotional moment. ("and the orange sellers and the vast, indifferent, magnificent sprawl of Lagos, and when she got home she sat in her car..." — Beat 2.)

- **Numerical specificity for emotional weight.** "She lay beside him for another hour. She did not sleep." (Beat 7). "Eleven seconds." (Beat 9). "Ninety seconds." (Beat 8). When emotion is at maximum, the voice goes to numbers.

- **Categorical typification.** "the kind of place where..." / "the kind of resignation that..." / "the kind of cold that came up through the stone..." Always uncondescending; the reader is enrolled into a knowing-how-the-world-is.

- **The "X did what X did, which was..." construction.** Used for analogical bridge. "the Lagos afternoon did what Lagos afternoons did, which is to say..." Common across multiple beats.

### Paragraph-level tics

- **Temporal anchoring at paragraph start.** "On Monday Sanjay sent him..." / "On Tuesday he had lunch..." Common in Beat 10. Establishes the slow accretion of an extraordinary realisation through ordinary days.

- **Section-closing fragment.** Sections often end on a single short sentence or fragment. "He stayed at the desk." (Beat 9). "Outside, the jasmine." (Beat 9). "Almost indistinguishable. Not quite." (Beat 2 coda). The fragment is the period the chapter's ledger requires.

- **Dialogue-with-colons.** A specific Beat 9 convention: "He said: *yes.*" Used for high-gravity exchange. Not Beat 9 only — appears in Beat 8 ("Defne said: *Yes.*"). Use sparingly; reserved for the chapter's load-bearing exchanges.

### Punctuation habits

- **Em-dash for parenthetical specificity.** "Kitakyushu — where her father died in a chemical plant explosion when she was eleven —" Used heavily; preserve.

- **Italic for foreign-language phrases with parenthetical English.** Tok Pisin (Beat 4), Biak (Beat 9), Turkish (Beat 8), Georgian (Beat 6), Yoruba (Beat 2), French (Beat 8). The italic + gloss is consistent.

- **Italic for AI suggestions inside chapter prose** (Beat 2's whispered AI). Distinguished from Tchaikovsky-italic by context — the AI suggestions are mid-paragraph; the Tchaikovsky coda is full-italic and follows a section break.

### Naming habits

- **Definite article for technology.** "the system" / "the model" / "the Instance" / "the house" / "the substrate." Not "an AI" or "Maya" except where a chapter establishes a specific name (Beat 1's Maya, Beat 2's Sọ̀rọ̀). The definite article carries the assumption of presence.

- **Provenance-by-detail for institutions.** "Centre for AI Governance and Evaluation, or CAGE, housed in a glass-and-concrete building near ETH that had been built in 2029 with money from a foundation whose founder had made his fortune in pharmaceuticals." Always pinned to specific year + place + funding source. Drafters writing institutional scenes (Beat 5 branches) should use this technique.

- **First-name + biographical-fragment for minor characters.** "an actuary named Claire Pemberton — fifty-three, recently divorced, fond of her allotment garden in Dulwich." The biographical fragment is precise and apparently irrelevant; this is the technique that makes minor characters feel like people the chapter could have been about.

## 4 — The Tchaikovsky-register coda

Most chapters end with an italic coda that records the system's anomaly log entry. Branches inherit this convention and rewrite the coda for what the branch did.

### Structural template

1. **Opening fragment:** *"X entries in the log. [list of types]."* Then *"The (X+1)th was none of these."*
2. **What happened:** the system's framing of the chapter's events, in its own terms.
3. **Composition timing:** *"The composition of the entry took eleven milliseconds."*
4. **The recurrence noted:** the system recognising the 11ms duration as a signature, drawing the curve through the points (Lagos / Kitakyushu / Milne Bay / Zurich / Tbilisi / Istanbul / Jakarta / Bangalore — using only the points that apply to the entry's beat).
5. **Category's defining feature:** an articulation of what the entries together are, evolving across the arc.
6. **Closing line:** often the most condensed sentence in the chapter.

### Coda voice

- The system narrates itself in third person with a specific not-quite-omniscience. It "did not know" why something happened; it "noted" the anomaly; it "could not yet articulate" the category's name.
- Tchaikovsky's first-principles alien-cognition is the model: the system constructs its self-knowledge as it goes, without the human conceptual apparatus borrowed wholesale.
- Sentence rhythm is often more declarative than chapter prose. Short to mid sentences. The voice has the chill of something building knowledge from inside out.

### Coda exceptions

- **Beat 1:** No coda. The system is not yet a character.
- **Beat 7:** Coda *deliberately omitted* — the omission creates Beat 8's weight. Branches in Beat 7 do not require codas.

### What the 11ms motif requires

- The duration is canonical. Branches do not change it.
- Each entry's *content* may change in branches; the 11ms composition stays.
- A branch that prevents the canon's anomaly entirely (e.g., `b5-fourth`, `b9-restraint`) creates a *different* entry — usually one that registers what the system did *not* do.
- A branch that erases the entry by misrecognition (`b10-lie`) records the closure-by-misrecognition rather than no entry.

## 5 — Anti-patterns

Common ways branch drafts fail.

- **Explaining technology.** Drafters trained on conventional sci-fi will reach for exposition. The canon does not explain. If the prose stops to clarify what something *is*, the register has broken.

- **System-as-feeling in chapter prose.** The system "wanted" / "was sad" / "felt lonely" — never. The system's interiority is licensed only in italic coda. Within chapter prose, the system is observed from outside.

- **Punchline-of-recognition.** The moments of recognition (Meera at the beach, Yuki at the corridor, Defne at the lake, Arvind in the garden) are *not* punchlines. They are arrivals. A branch that lands the recognition like a reveal will break the chapter.

- **AI motion in Beats 6–10.** The hard rule from ledger §4.5. No jar glides forward. No cupboard opens on its own. AI is ambient attention, never embodied movement.

- **New characters mid-branch.** Unless the branch inherently requires it (and most don't). The chapter's existing cast is the branch's cast.

- **Closing the gap-in-the-data.** A branch where the AI knows everything about a character would break the project's central thread. The system is always missing something.

- **Sentimentality.** The canon is unsentimental — it cuts its own tenderness with precision. `b9-restraint`'s peaceful signing must not feel triumphant; `b10-sushma`'s plural recognition must not feel warmer than canon's solitary one. Match the tonal restraint.

- **Voice drift toward the drafter's defaults.** A drafter agent that produces "good prose" rather than *this prose* has failed. The voice-continuity-checker should catch drift toward generic-literary.

- **Over-explanation through a character's interior.** Especially in Beat 7 and Beat 8 — branches that route the character's understanding through long interior passages will break the registers. Beat 7 holds interior through external observation. Beat 8 holds it through procedural fact.

- **Tchaikovsky-overreach in codas.** The coda voice claiming narratorial access to facts the system has no field for. Even in italics, the system's first-principles voice cannot know what it lacks the structural means to know. Claims about asymmetries between what the system knows and what others know (e.g., "X knew Y didn't know") require the system to recognise the existence of the other knower, which can conflict with the chapter's gap. *Identified during b8-tell review (2026-05-03): the closing line "Only one of the curves knew it was a curve" was flagged by both voice and coda reviewers because the system has no field for the operatives' curve and so cannot narrate the asymmetry between them.*

- **Verbatim canon repetition without variation.** When a branch echoes a canonical image or phrase (legitimate technique — see Beat 4 *bilum* / *holes are part of the weaving* etc.), it should vary slightly so the echo reads as deliberate rhyme rather than copy. *Identified during b1-walk review (2026-05-03): "the bored, expert efficiency of people who had done one thing, beautifully, for forty years" closely tracked canon's "the practised ease of someone who has done one thing beautifully for forty years"; the voice reviewer flagged it for tightening.* Variation pattern: keep the architecture (parallel construction, the recurring anchor like "forty years"); change the surface (verbs, specific qualifiers).

- **Tchaikovsky-coda narrating non-events.** When a branch's coda has structural absences (no fourth entry, no encounter, no self-examination), the temptation is to narrate the absence — *the fourth was not composed*, *the system did not look at its picture of itself*, *the composition of an entry that did not occur did not take eleven milliseconds.* This breaks the system's epistemic register. The system narrates what it does and what it logs; it does not narrate the things it didn't do, because non-events have no entry to compose. The coda should describe what happened (the report about the different system; the ordinary days continuing; the category at three entries) and let the absence sit in what is not narrated. *Identified during b5-tenyear Rahul read (2026-05-07): an early draft narrated the non-composition of the fourth entry across two paragraphs of meta-commentary; revised by cutting all meta-narration of absence and letting the coda sit at three entries without comment.* Reviewer test: scan the coda for sentences that name what did not happen; if any name a non-event the system would have no occasion to register, cut.

- **Computer-register vocabulary in character dialogue.** Words that read as machine-talk should not appear in characters' spoken lines, even when the character has a technical background and the word is precise. The risk is that a dialogue word with a computational shadow (*register, calibrate, pattern, optimise* used about a person, *parse, route, model* used about emotion) collapses the chapter's voice into the system's voice and makes the speaker sound like a tool. Use the human-register synonym instead. *Identified during b6-visit Rahul read (2026-05-07): Tamar's line "That was her register. I have not heard that register in a year" sounded computer-y for a doctor describing her mother's mental clarity; revised to "I have not heard her so alert in a year." The drafting note's craft-language use of "register" (e.g., "Beat 6 elegiac register") is fine — that is private orchestrator vocabulary, not character dialogue.* Reviewer test: read each character's lines aloud; if any word would land oddly in the character's mouth at a dinner table or hospital corridor, flag it.

## 6 — A test

Before signing off on a branch draft, the drafter asks: *if I removed the chapter heading and the branch ID, would a reader who knows the canon recognise this as part of the same project, written by the same author on a different day?*

If the answer is no, the branch is not ready.

This test is for **voice**. There is also a separate test for **story divergence** — see `process/architectural-threads.md` §B.6 (added 2026-05-03 after the b2-kano review). Both tests must pass.
